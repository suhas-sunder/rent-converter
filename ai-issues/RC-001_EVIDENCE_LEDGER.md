## Confirmed implementation weaknesses and investigation targets

1. Home worked example states $2,000 every four weeks is $26,000/year and $2,166.67/month. The calculator annualizes 365/28 and produces $26,071.43/year and $2,172.62/month. Distinguish 13 payments over 364 days from a 365-day rate equivalent; do not change protected math to fit prose.
2. Shared `rentMath.parseMoneyToCents` strips unsupported characters: `1e3` becomes 13 and `(100)` becomes positive 100. Home and several route-local parsers use similar cleaning. These are source-confirmed input reinterpretation defects; repair only with supported-format regression matrices. Protected routes need comprehensive tests.
3. Paycheck tool divides annual rent by fixed 52/26/24/12 counts. It explicitly excludes pay dates, so it cannot determine whether the next bill is funded before its deadline. A dated reserve workflow is substantive additional value, not a text expansion.
4. Budget tool subtracts rent and aggregate expenses from gross income. It labels this basis, but spendable money requires net-income/expense planning; do not imply the displayed remainder is actual disposable cash.
5. Generated income parser treats `1.234` as a thousands group but route-local decimal parsers may treat it as a decimal. Preserve existing supported formats while making ambiguity explicit; do not homogenize silently.
6. Schedule generation uses an exclusive calculated lease-end boundary. Source copy says so, but agreement end-day semantics and final partial payments need scenario review before changing behavior.
