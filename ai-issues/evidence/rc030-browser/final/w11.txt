Monthly pay repeats the day of your next payday. Semimonthly pay uses the two days entered. A day missing from a
shorter month moves to month-end; coincident dates count once. Weekly and biweekly pay repeat every 7 and 14 days.
Weekends, bank holidays and deposit delays are not adjusted. Check your employer’s schedule. Extra cents go to the
earliest paydays so the reserves add up exactly. This plan stays in this tab and is included when you print with this section
open.
Biweekly means every 14 days; semimonthly means two dates each month. The annual comparison uses 26 and 24
paychecks respectively, while this plan counts the entered dates. Some calendar years contain 27 biweekly paydays. Four-
week rent covers 28 days, not a calendar month. Currency changes labels only, not exchange rates. This is a planning
estimate, not financial advice.

Annual comparison factors
Rent rows use period equivalents: 12 calendar months, or a 365-day year divided by 28 days or 7
days. Pay rows use fixed annual budget assumptions of 52, 26, 24 or 12 paychecks. Neither set
counts actual dated payments. Use the date-based plan above to count paydays before your
rent deadline.
